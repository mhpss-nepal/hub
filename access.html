Who can see the records

Filling in a form needs nothing at all. Reading what has been filled in needs to be granted by name. This page is where that is granted — the explanation to share with partners is here.

Nothing needed

Field workers filling forms

They open the link, fill the form, and save. No account, no email, no password. A worker in a shelter with a borrowed phone can report without anyone setting them up first — that is the whole point, and nothing on this page changes it.

Granted by name

Anyone reading the records

Reading is a coordination act, so the database asks who you are and whether you were given access. Signing in is not enough on its own: an account with no grant here sees nothing.

What each level can do

Why access is granted by email address rather than by account.
  • You type the person's address and pick a level. There is no user ID to copy between console tabs, which is where this kind of thing usually goes wrong.
  • Nobody has to choose, type, or share a password — including you. You never hold anyone else's credentials, so nothing traces back to you if an account is later misused.
  • Every grant records who made it and when, on the server's clock. The grant itself is evidence.
  • The trade-off, stated plainly: if someone changes the email address they sign in with, their access stops until it is granted again.
